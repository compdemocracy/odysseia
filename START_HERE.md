# Start Here

## Scope of Work

Our task today is create a wholly new set of public facing pages for the Polis project from Computational Democracy. These pages should be "marketing" and educational -- tell a compelling "story" about what polis is, how it is used, and which tier or version of the product is best for you (the audience). Think modern SaaS startup "lander" meets civic tech nonprofit.

## Step Three

Consider these user types. How can we serve each of them? How should information be organized to best chaperone each user type through their respective flow or journey?

- Hobbyist / Small-scale user. This could be a curious individual or small organization (eg a school, a local government org, a club). They should be encouraged to set up and use the free tier right away.

- Researcher / Data Scientist. Academic interest, possibly overlapping with hobbyist or small org, but with a much higher appetite for data science (the "nerdy stuff"). We love these. These are our people. Some folks use polis in their Ph.D. work. They might contribute important knowledge back to us or to the community at large. They want details about the algorithms, interactive charts, access to data sets. And they should be able to easily find these things.

- Large org. / state actor. This is like Finland, the UK, the UN, California. They want to vet polis for their specific needs. They want to know if Computational Democracy is trustworthy and responsive (yes to both). They want details about compliance (privacy, GDPR, EU requirements, etc) and should be quickly reassured and impressed by our compliance and track record. We want to convert these actors into Polis Pro customers.

- DIY-ers / open-source contributors. Least concern. We love them but they need very little hand-holding and are not the target of our marketing per se. They should find our github repo, wiki, and other already published documentation.

- Investors / donors. As a non-profit we solicit grant contributions. Sometimes these are from deep-pocketed tech giants (Google, Meta, OpenAI). This is a major target. But it's not a traditional sales funnel; they are not targets for customer conversion per se. The whole site should impress them. They click around and say "ok, wow, these guysa are really doing it". This is the "linear.app vibes" we want. We are modern, smart, capable, and unique. Check us out. And give us money. Small donors should find a "Donate Now" button. Large donors should talk to us.

## Step Four

Consider the overall style and "vibe". This is a rough draft and we will certainly iterate and adapt. Let me specify what we don't want. We don't want to look too "for-profit SaaS". As such we want a light mode only, no dark mode (unlike linear). But we also don't want to come across as a stodgy nonprofit bureaucracy. And we certainly don't want to resemble a cookie-cutter "marketing lander template" with three columns and a slide carousel. We are ok looking scrappy, organic, understaffed, pragmatic, and nerdy. Space Age Civic Tech mood. Our favorite color is blue. Specifically `#03a9f4`. We are technically a SaaS. A fremium nonprofit SaaS for the public good.

## Step Five

Consider the variety of content. This is a moving target and open to creativity. Some items here may include:

- Use cases and testimonials
- Big Picture "elevator pitch" (what is this and how does it work)
- Media about polis
- White papers and academic / scientific stuff
- Information about The Computational Democracy Project
- Interactive data visualizations
- Guides (Quick Start, tips)
- Comparison of "tiers"
  - Polis 2.0 (free for everyone, basic support)
  - Polis Pro (paid subscription, extra features and support)
  - Self-Hosted (open-source DIY, AGPL, no formal support)
- Calls to action
  - Start a conversation now
  - Donate
  - Contact us
  - Sign up for Pro
  - Open an Issue

## Step Six

Prototype some wireframes.

This directory contains a basic scaffold for for an Astro static content site (codenamed odysseia). Tailwind is included. This is a sandbox for templating and designing the pages. Nothing is sacred, just start building. Details will be filled in later.

Notice I did not include any exhaustive list of features, pricing specifics, media links, or pre-written copy. All of these details will be amended later. We can ad-lib and use placeholders or just make things up right now. The main thing today is nail the overall style, design, and "vibe".

Don't one-shot this. We will iterate, add new details, pivot, incorporate feedback, and adapt. Ideas, plans, todo lists, etc can be created and added to the "docs/" folder as needed.
